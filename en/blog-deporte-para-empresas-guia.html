How to set up corporate sport: a step-by-step guide.

Everything you need to decide before launching a corporate sport program, in five steps.

More and more companies want to offer sport to their staff, but many get stuck at the "we should do something" stage. The reason is almost never budget: it's not knowing where to start. This guide covers the real decisions you need to make to launch a corporate sport program that works long-term, not just for the first few weeks.

In this guide

01. The goal 02. The format 03. The space 04. The frequency 05. Measuring results
01

Define the program's goal

Setting up a program to reduce stress in a burnt-out team is not the same as one to strengthen cohesion in a workforce that just merged, or one designed as a perk to attract talent. The goal shapes the format, the frequency and even the tone of the sessions, so it's worth getting clear on it before choosing anything else.

02

Choose the format: physical activity, team building, or both

Regular physical activity (yoga, HIIT, functional training) has the biggest impact on day-to-day health and energy. Sport-based team building brings more cohesion than physical performance. Most programs that work combine both: a weekly base of physical activity, plus the occasional event or challenge that breaks the routine.

03

Decide where you'll train

There's no need to rent a new venue or renovate anything. A large meeting room or a common area in the office works for most disciplines. If the company or business park already has its own gym, you can use it directly. And for one-off events, a nearby park or rooftop also works.

"What makes the difference isn't the discipline you choose, it's consistency."

04

How often to schedule sessions

An occasional one-off session brings little real change. What makes the difference is consistency: 45- to 60-minute sessions, once or several times a week, at a fixed time the team can plan around in advance. From there, you can add monthly talks or quarterly events.

05

How to measure whether it's working

Beyond attendance, it's worth doing simple monthly tracking: how many people keep coming back, what feedback they give about the format, and whether there's any change in indicators like absenteeism or team atmosphere. Adjusting the program every month, instead of waiting for the annual renewal, is what stops it from becoming a passing trend.
